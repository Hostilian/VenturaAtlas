Venture Bets

A venture bet is a bounded commitment to learn or create value from an idea. One idea can produce multiple bets with radically different cost, time, and reversibility. Prefer reversible bets when uncertainty is high. Require stronger evidence before irreversible commitments.

Forecast Registry

A probability is not trustworthy because it came from a confident source or has two decimal places. Performance must be measured after outcomes resolve. A forecast is a public commitment — preserved immutably so hindsight does not rewrite history. Calibration requires many resolved forecasts. Do not draw conclusions from small samples.

Brier score: (probability − outcome)². Lower is better. A naive 50% baseline scores 0.25 always.

Strategic Memory

A snapshot preserves: selected bets, allocations, active forecasts, constraints, and assumptions — all at a specific moment. Later review can ask honestly: Did we reject this for the right reason? Did our forecasts reflect what we actually knew? Which assumptions failed? Which signals were noise?

Do not optimize for looking right in hindsight. Optimize for making better future decisions.

ORBIT Design Principles
Portfolio value ≠ sum of idea scores

A mediocre standalone venture can be a superb stepping stone. Two strong ideas can form a weak portfolio if their risks are identical.

Prefer reversible actions under uncertainty

The point of an inexpensive early action may not be immediate profit. It buys the right — but not obligation — to make a larger commitment after uncertainty falls.

Attention is a resource

Three unrelated 10-hour projects may impose greater cognitive burden than one 30-hour project. Model it.

Reserve capacity has value

Allocating 100% of time/capital automatically is a mistake. Uncommitted reserve responds to new information.

A failed bet can have high quality

A failure is high-quality if it was cheap, resolved important uncertainty, prevented larger loss, and created reusable learning.

Calibration must face reality

A probability is not useful because it sounds intelligent. Its predictions must resolve. Brier scores accumulate over many questions — sample size matters.
