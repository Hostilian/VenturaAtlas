# OMEGA-XIX Track B — audit log

## Step 3 verdict — mixed pass, with a hard ceiling

At eight ideas, CHESSBOARD’s rigor remained operational: source URLs were required, epistemic labels were preserved, and each snapshot included control points, dependencies, moat/anti-moat falsifiers, and a next action. The exercise also exposed the ceiling. The final regulatory ideas had credible adjacent platforms and standards but no evidence that an independent replay/audit wedge beats an incumbent or that a buyer will pay. The process therefore passes as a small research audit and fails as a license for 324-item production.

## Source/research notes

- Evaluation: LangSmith docs support offline/online evaluation and trajectory/tool inspection; Braintrust documents systematic evaluation; DeepEval documents OSS agent/tool/conversational metrics; Humanloop documents dataset/evaluator comparison; OpenAI Guardrails documents agentic safety checks.
- Commerce data: Akeneo, Syndigo, Productsup, and Google Merchant Center document centralized product data, validation, enrichment, syndication, or product-data controls.
- Research lineage: Code Ocean, Whole Tale, Renku, and Zenodo document executable environments, provenance, preservation, and reproducible artifacts.
- Marketplaces: Sharetribe, Mirakl, Arcadier, and Shopify document configurable marketplace or app-platform infrastructure.
- E-invoicing: Avalara, Sovos, Pagero, and the European Commission document compliance APIs, validation, routing, archives, ERP connectivity, and public conformance tools.
- Accessibility: Deque axe, AudioEye, and W3C document automated/manual testing, expert audits, and WCAG 2.1 as a stable reference standard.

These observations support actor existence and documented capability only. They do not support market share, superiority, customer count, pricing, legal sufficiency, or willingness to pay unless explicitly stated and separately verified.

## What held

- Exactly eight ideas, not a generated corpus.
- Six categories, with two AI-evaluation ideas retained because their control/dependency structures differ (language coverage vs journey adherence).
- Every named actor has a URL.
- No mechanism was promoted to fact merely because a competitor exists.
- Every moat and anti-moat has an explicit falsifier.
- Every snapshot ends with a bounded paid or behaviorally binding next action.

## Where the method strained

- **Idea 5 (Result Lineage):** adjacent reproducibility platforms are strong, but claim-level release gating is not established by their product pages. The gap is explicit and must remain `UNKNOWN`.
- **Idea 7 (Invoice Replay):** regulation creates urgency, but Avalara/Sovos/Pagero and public EU tooling control much of the path. End-to-end buyer-system replay is a testable wedge, not an observed moat.
- **Idea 8 (EAA Audit):** WCAG and incumbent audit tooling are clear; EAA applicability and legal defensibility are jurisdictional questions requiring specialist review. No legal claim was allowed into the snapshot.

The shortcuts that would have broken rigor were avoided: no invented competitor, no uncited market-size claim, no silent score overwrite, no automatic “green gate” treated as validation, and no background-worker claim. The strain is enough to block scaling without a new review budget.

## Human-only blockers

The FactBounty transaction, consent, payment, delivery, and cohort decision cannot be completed by repository research. Track A’s ownership and score decisions also remain pending. Source freshness and legal review are required before any public commercial or compliance statement.
