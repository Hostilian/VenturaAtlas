## Executive verdict

The MERCURY MVP is implemented as a browser-local commercial-reality workspace integrated with Decision Studio. It can represent a narrow segment, buying trigger, buyer/account, current alternative, offer and pricing hypothesis, channel, interaction, objection, opportunity history, payment/refund, value, renewal, expansion, referral, and evidence-linked hypothesis change. A separate private receipt validator exists for stronger production-event evidence.

The current FactBounty brief remains **C0 — hypothetical buyer**. Repository validation reports zero identified organizations, zero conversations, zero paying organizations, zero recorded revenue, and no accessible external commercial-outcome receipt directory. Neither the UI nor its passing fixtures creates customers, revenue, interviews, retention, or market validation.

MERCURY is not a CRM, email sender, automated outreach agent, always-on worker, or production payment verifier. Shared mode, multi-venture portfolio views, cohort retention, commercial search, connectors, and advanced cross-venture intelligence remain future work.

## A. Commercial architecture added

Implemented three explicit layers:

1. A schema-versioned local workspace (`mercury-workspace` 1.1.0).
2. Pure derived summaries for funnel, evidence ladder, objections, losses, revenue/refunds, activation, renewal, and segment performance.
3. A separate private `commercial-outcome-receipt` chain for stronger digest-bound, production-only transactional and outcome evidence.

The local workspace is operator-attested and deliberately labeled as not independently verified. External receipts distinguish operator attestations from independently verifiable evidence and reject mock, sandbox, simulated, or test transaction environments.

## D. Segment model

Implemented: parent segment, operational description, buyer and user roles, budget owner/source, why-now hypothesis, current alternatives, reachability, and hypothesis status. Segment performance separates organizations, attempts, conversations, qualified, offered, pilot, paid, activated, renewed, and lost counts.

Limitation: no automated segment discovery or causal inference; small-N percentages are intentionally avoided.

## E. Buyer/buying-committee model

Implemented role arrays, budget owner/source, and buyer/account actor types for organization, individual account, household, and marketplace participant.

Limitation: no person-level committee graph or contact fields. Personal contact details and transcripts belong in an approved private system and are referenced non-identifyingly.

## F. Trigger model

Implemented trigger type, observable description, detectability, urgency window, message implication, segment binding, and status. Triggers remain hypotheses until interactions support them.

## G. Current-alternative model

Implemented as explicit segment alternatives. The current FactBounty brief lists seller questions, review search, accepting return risk, and abandoning the purchase. MERCURY does not infer dissatisfaction or switching intent.

## H. Offer/pricing system

Implemented concrete deliverable, scope, motion, price amount/currency/basis, time-to-value, pilot type, and evidence status. `PAID` pricing status cannot be manually asserted; payment is derived from commercial events. Invoice issuance is not revenue. Opportunity offer/commitment signals must reference an offer-linked opportunity that already reached OFFERED.

## I. Channel system

Implemented segment-bound channel, motion, access path, and hypothesis/observed/unknown/rejected status. Interactions require an explicit channel, including an explicit UNKNOWN channel after legacy migration.

No automated outreach exists. The active `idea-061` plan now limits contact to permitted, non-repetitive, moderator-approved or opt-in paths.

## J. Private organization/prospect model

Implemented minimal buyer/account record, actor type, segment, real/synthetic class, derived stage, reachability basis, private evidence reference, evidence class, and timestamp. Legacy records migrate as `UNVERIFIED_LEGACY` and cannot earn C1 until reviewed. Cascade deletion removes dependent interactions, opportunities, and commercial events.

## K. Interaction/customer-research model

Implemented contact attempt, conversation, follow-up, pilot review, and loss review; observed outcome; fact list; objections; objection categories; supported signals; claims not earned; channel; optional opportunity; and private evidence reference.

No-reply/contact-attempt records cannot carry confirmed signals. Evaluation/offer/commitment signals require a qualified or next-step outcome. Offer acceptance and commitment additionally require the same buyer/segment and a concrete offer already recorded as offered.

Consent and source bytes remain outside this compact workspace; the UI instructs the operator to retain consented evidence privately. MERCURY does not claim that a text reference proves consent.

## L. Opportunity/pipeline model

New opportunities always start at IDENTIFIED. Manual transitions are constrained and evidence-referenced. CONTACTED requires a contact attempt; CONVERSATION requires a conversation; QUALIFIED requires a qualified outcome; OFFERED/PILOT require a linked offer. PAID, ACTIVE, and RENEWED are event-derived. Stage history is append-only and supports ever-reached funnel counts after a loss.

## M. Objection engine

Implemented free-text observed objections plus a fixed taxonomy: budget, price, urgency, authority, trust, fit, procurement, timing, competitor, status quo, other, and unknown. Aggregates are derived by category and segment; the system does not infer hidden motives.

## N. Win/loss system

Loss requires an evidence reference and reason. Loss analytics retain prior funnel history. “Win” is not a free-form label: collected payment produces PAID, observed value produces ACTIVE, and renewal produces RENEWED. A fully refunded buyer remains historically paid but is separated from currently net-paying buyers.

## O. Activation/retention system

Local operator events cover payment, refund, value achieved, renewed, expanded, and referred. Causal ordering, opportunity identity, chronology, currency, refund cap, and lifecycle monotonicity are validated. The stronger external receipt layer additionally requires production transaction evidence and independent value evidence; repeat payment requires prior value and a later commitment.

Limitation: no cohort retention analysis, MRR/ARR inference, or automatic product telemetry import.

## P. Commercial analytics

Implemented counts for real buyer records, attested reachability, attempts, conversations, qualification, offers, explicit pilots, gross historical payers, net payers, refunded buyers, activation, renewal, loss, objections, loss reasons, segment performance, and net collected amount by currency. Invoices are excluded. Synthetic records are excluded.

## Q. GTM/Customer Lab UI

Implemented at `docs/mercury.html` with responsive forms, scoreboard, current commercial brief, constrained pipeline transitions, buyer/account history, objection/loss learning, segment comparison, hypothesis-change journal, import/export, legacy review, cascade deletion, accessible live status, and explicit next human action.

The UI is static-first, browser-local, and offline-cached. It does not load a remote commercial SDK.

## R. Private/public boundaries

Limitation: local browser storage is not encrypted by this application. Device/browser security and approved evidence storage remain operator responsibilities.
