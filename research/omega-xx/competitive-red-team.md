# Competitive red team

## eFTI Conformance Gateway — reasons it should not exist

1. eFTI platforms may bundle validation free. 2. Remaining specifications may remove uncertainty. 3. Certification may not permit modular tooling. 4. TMS vendors may buy one platform. 5. Data mappings may be simple. 6. National authority implementations may diverge. 7. Accredited assessors may provide equivalent packs. 8. Security liability may make customers build in-house. 9. The long tail may lack budget. 10. Platform providers may become the integration standard.

## EUDR API Bridge — reasons it should not exist

1. ERP vendors may ship native connectors. 2. Consultants may absorb API work. 3. Official UI may be adequate. 4. API access may remain unstable. 5. API terms may restrict intermediaries. 6. Geolocation vendors may own the workflow. 7. Operators may submit low volumes manually. 8. The deadline may be delayed. 9. A dominant traceability vendor may bundle it. 10. Support liability may exceed subscription value.

## DPP Runtime — reasons it should not exist

1. Existing DPP vendors already expose APIs. 2. The Commission provides a free semantic repository. 3. ERP/PIM vendors may build direct adapters. 4. Sector delegated acts may fragment the market. 5. Registry traffic may be low. 6. Standards may be incomplete. 7. Customers may buy an end-to-end DPP suite. 8. Service-provider rules may add certification overhead. 9. GS1 tooling may cover identifiers/data carriers. 10. A public registry may commoditise the adapter.

## Kill tests

Kill the wedge when three independent target interviews confirm that the exact integration/conformance layer is bundled, already outsourced to a named incumbent, or not budgeted.
