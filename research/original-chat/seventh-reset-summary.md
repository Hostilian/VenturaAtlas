# Executive result

## Provisional winner: Result Lineage Release Gate

An author or laboratory uploads:

- the manuscript;
- figures and tables;
- data and metadata;
- code, notebooks, spreadsheets, or queries;
- environment and dependency files;
- repository deposit draft;
- data/software availability statements;
- contribution and disclosure statements.

The product creates an explicit graph from each reported result to the objects that produced and release it:

> manuscript claim → figure/table/number → source data → transformation/analysis → environment/version → repository object → persistent identifier → availability statement

The release fails when a required edge is missing, contradictory, inaccessible, non-releasable, identity-leaking, or points to the wrong version.

The product does **not** certify that the science is true, detect misconduct conclusively, replace peer review, or guarantee journal acceptance. It verifies internal release lineage and packages evidence for human review.

Initial offer:

> Pre-submission result-lineage audit for one empirical or computational manuscript. We map every figure, table, and major numeric result to its source files and release objects, identify broken or contradictory links, and deliver a versioned release manifest. Founding price: €79.

# Round 1 — Structural-change scan

- NIH requires a simplified 2026 Data Management and Sharing Plan format for due dates on or after 25 May 2026, while the underlying sharing policy remains in force.
- Funders and journals increasingly require data availability statements, repository identifiers, software/code sharing, privacy justification, and evidence of actual deposit.
- Artifact-evaluation tracks require README files, smoke tests, reproduction instructions, claim-to-artifact descriptions, archival repositories, and persistent identifiers.
- The World Bank requires reproducibility packages for empirical Policy Research Working Papers and verifies packages before publishing a DOI-linked version of record.
- Computational studies continue to find substantial execution failure caused by undeclared dependencies, invalid paths, environment drift, non-determinism, and missing inputs.
- Crossref and DataCite published 2026 guidance positioning contributor, version, correction, funding, rights, reference, and relationship metadata as research-integrity infrastructure.
- Fabricated references and AI-assisted writing increase the cost of citation verification and pre-submission quality control.
- Peer-review systems are under capacity pressure, increasing incentives to move routine and evidence-based checks earlier in the workflow.
- Conferences maintain strict, changing rules on anonymity, authorship, supplements, page limits, disclosure, and linked code; small violations can cause desk rejection.
- Scientific publishers increasingly require accessible figures, alt text, long descriptions, tagged supplements, readable labels, units, and colour-independent meaning.
- Open-access rights-retention, funder policies, APC agreements, repository routes, and accepted-manuscript requirements remain fragmented.
- Research-software citation standards, CodeMeta, CITATION.cff, DataCite metadata, CRediT roles, and release DOIs are converging but not perfectly aligned.
- Retractions and corrections do not reliably propagate into reviews, public knowledge bases, or downstream citations.
- Human-participant data sharing requires more than mechanical de-identification; consent, controlled access, legal restrictions, misuse risk, and institutional review remain relevant.
- Integrated publisher screening products are emerging, proving demand while making a generic integrity dashboard a weak new entrant.

# Round 3 — Direct recurring problems

- **P-01:** A figure exists in the manuscript, but nobody can identify the exact data and script version that produced it.
- **P-02:** A reported number was copied from an intermediate spreadsheet and cannot be regenerated.
- **P-03:** A data availability statement claims availability while its link, identifier, file set, licence, or access conditions do not match.
- **P-04:** A repository release changes after verification, so the public package is no longer the one that was checked.
- **P-05:** A notebook works on the author's machine but fails in a clean environment.
- **P-06:** Software is cited by URL or paper while the exact release used remains unidentified.
- **P-07:** A manuscript references supplementary files that are missing, renamed, unreadable, or identity-revealing.
- **P-08:** Authors discover anonymity leaks in Git history, metadata, linked pages, or file paths after submission.
- **P-09:** Citations contain incorrect or fabricated metadata, or point to retracted or corrected works.
- **P-10:** Review responses lose track of which concern was addressed, where the evidence is, and what changed.
- **P-11:** Data-management plans are written for grants but do not become an operational workflow.
- **P-12:** Researchers struggle to select repositories and explain privacy or access restrictions consistently.
- **P-13:** Figures remain inaccessible because captions, labels, colour use, alt text, and long descriptions are treated separately.
- **P-14:** Authorship and contribution decisions are reconstructed only at submission time.
- **P-15:** A departing researcher leaves code, data, credentials ownership, and unresolved obligations fragmented.
- **P-16:** Qualitative claims and quotations lose their link to approved source, consent state, anonymization, and codebook version.
- **P-17:** Accepted manuscripts are not deposited under the correct rights-retention and funder route.
- **P-18:** Corrections to one scholarly object do not propagate to linked datasets, software, reviews, or internal knowledge.
- **P-19:** Research repositories accidentally expose secrets, personal data, licensed inputs, or restricted material.
- **P-20:** Researchers face numerous specialized checks but lack one human-readable release decision showing how the objects fit together.

# Round 8 — Failure and contradiction search

- Publisher-integrated suites may add cross-object lineage before a startup gains institutional distribution.
- Many researchers will not personally pay because compliance support is expected from their university or journal.
- A release graph can verify links and files but cannot establish that a scientific claim is valid.
- Automatically extracting claims and matching them to evidence can generate false confidence or noisy false positives.
- Research workflows differ greatly across disciplines, making a universal schema difficult.
- Sensitive or proprietary work cannot be uploaded to an external service.
- Computational verification may require expensive hardware, licensed software, restricted data, or days of runtime.
- Repository APIs, conference policies, funder rules, and publisher instructions change frequently.
- Labs may postpone release preparation until immediately before a deadline, when onboarding tolerance is low.
- Open-source checklists and local scripts can satisfy technically mature teams.
- Institutional procurement cycles are slow and require security, accessibility, privacy, and legal review.
- Per-manuscript author revenue may be low relative to support burden.
- The product may become a feature inside an electronic lab notebook, writing platform, repository, or publisher submission system.
- Citation verification cannot determine whether a source genuinely supports the author's interpretation without expert review.
- Privacy red teaming, authorship decisions, licence interpretation, and research-integrity conclusions require qualified humans.

# Deep Investigation — Result Lineage Release Gate

## Customer

- Computational or empirical researchers approaching submission.
- Lab managers and principal investigators who need a minimum release standard.
- Research software engineers, data stewards, librarians, and scientific editors supporting many teams.
- Publishers or conferences that want an author-owned evidence packet without outsourcing editorial judgment.

**The customer pays because** missing or contradictory release objects can cause desk rejection, publication delay, unusable artifacts, corrections, repeated staff work, and reputational harm.

**The idea satisfies the customer by** making every released result inspectable from manuscript statement to source and preserving the exact approved version.

## Product workflow

1. Import manuscript, repository/folder, figures, tables, supplements, statements, and planned deposit.
2. Extract figure/table labels, in-text numerical results, data/software statements, citations, and disclosures.
3. Generate a versioned release graph and manifest.
4. Flag broken references, missing code files, unlinked numbers, non-reproducible scripts, and anonymity leaks.
5. Provide actionable guidance and export a verified release evidence package.
