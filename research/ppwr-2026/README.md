# PPWR signal audit — 21 August 2026

Decision: **DO NOT REPLACE THE WINNER. DO NOT CREATE A NEW CANONICAL IDEA.**

The signal contains a real, current forcing function and unusually good fragmentation evidence. The proposed conclusion does not survive legal-timing, penalty, competition, Czech-market, or corpus-deduplication checks.

## What is real

- Regulation (EU) 2025/40 generally applies from 12 August 2026.
- Articles 44 and 45 create country-level producer registration/EPR duties and verification duties for covered online platforms and fulfilment service providers.
- Amazon-commissioned research found 64 unique registration fields across ten Member States, only four universally required, with material authentication, language, offline-process, dual-registration, and API gaps.
- The law creates high administrative burden for cross-border distance sellers and a potentially meaningful market-access consequence.

## What was wrong or overstated

- **“Immediate delisting across all 27 Member States from 12 August” is not established.** Article 45 requires information before allowing producers to use covered platform services, but Article 44's harmonised registers are phased. The Commission says Member States get 18 months after the implementing act to establish them and first reporting under the harmonised rules is due 1 June 2030. Existing national regimes and platform rollout differ.
- Article 45(6) imposes a **best-efforts assessment** on online platforms. The explicit “swiftly suspend” language in Article 45(8) is directed at fulfilment service providers after an unremedied information problem. Treating every marketplace seller as automatically delisted on the application date overstates the text.
- A third-country producer is not automatically required by PPWR itself to appoint an authorised representative in every Member State. Article 45(3) says Member States **may** require this. EU-established cross-border distance sellers are covered by the mandatory first sentence; the failed Omnibus suspension proposal did not change that.
- “Most countries have a zero-kilogram threshold” is not demonstrated by the supplied evidence. Registration, reporting, fee, micro-enterprise, and national exemptions must be separated.
- The penalty examples mix older national regimes with PPWR and are materially unreliable: Germany's Packaging Act contains prohibition and fines up to €200,000 for specified violations, not the claimed €600,000; Spain's €600,000 ceiling is tied to hazardous-waste cases in its general waste law; the Irish packaging regulations found provide up to €500,000 and imprisonment, not €15 million.
- The cheap DIY tier is not thin enough to claim whitespace. Multiple live products already offer free or €19–€49 self-service entry tiers.
- The Czech entity is EKO-KOM and the Ministry's List of Subjects—not “ČOV.” EKO-KOM already provides substantial English guidance and foreign-company onboarding.

## Corpus disposition

This proposal is a **feature/segment hypothesis inside the existing staged PackGate / PackProof family**, not a new concept. The narrow surviving question is whether sellers will pay for a country-routing and evidence-preflight layer that remains useful after free guidance, marketplaces, PROs, and low-cost self-service products are considered.
